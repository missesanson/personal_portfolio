Editing: Before and After

A practical demonstration of how structural and line editing can make a paragraph clearer without erasing its meaning.

RoleStructural edit, line edit and commentary

The original

Due to the fact that modern students are now exposed to a very large amount of information from many different sources online, it is becoming increasingly important in today's world for schools to teach critical thinking skills because otherwise students may not be able to know whether the information that they are seeing is reliable, biased, inaccurate or potentially misleading in some way.

The point is sensible, but the sentence carries too many ideas at once. Several phrases repeat meaning without adding precision.

The edited version

Students now encounter more information than they can reasonably verify. Schools should therefore teach critical thinking as a practical skill: how to test a source, recognise bias and distinguish uncertainty from misinformation.

What changed

1. The cause became direct

“Due to the fact that” became a clear statement about the situation. The revision starts with the reader's problem rather than a long introductory phrase.

2. The claim became more specific

“Critical thinking skills” can mean almost anything. The edited version names three actions the student should be able to perform.

3. Repetition became structure

The original lists “reliable, biased, inaccurate or potentially misleading.” Some of these ideas overlap. The revision groups them into source quality, bias and uncertainty.

4. The rhythm improved

One long sentence became two sentences with different jobs. The first defines the problem. The second states the response.

A heavier rewrite

When a stronger editorial voice is appropriate, the same idea could become:

The internet does not merely give students answers; it gives them claims competing to look like answers. Education must teach them how to inspect those claims—who made them, what evidence supports them and how confidently they should be believed.

This version is more memorable, but it also adds a stronger voice. Whether it is better depends on the purpose of the piece.

The principle

Editing is not the search for the fanciest sentence. It is the search for the sentence that performs the intended job with the least unnecessary friction.
