Founder LinkedIn Ghostwriting Set

Three voice-matched posts for a fictional founder writing about leadership, product decisions and useful ambition.

Voice brief

The fictional founder is direct, reflective and practical. He avoids exaggerated success stories and prefers observations drawn from ordinary work.

Post 1: The decision log

We started keeping a decision log because our meetings were becoming too efficient.

That sounds like a strange problem.

We would discuss something, agree quickly and move on. Two months later, the decision still existed—but the reasoning had disappeared.

Then the same questions returned:

  • What did we know at the time?
  • Which trade-off did we accept?
  • What would make us reconsider?

Now every meaningful decision gets three lines:

  1. What we decided
  2. Why we decided it
  3. What evidence would change our mind

The log has not made us slower. It has stopped us from repeating old arguments without realising it.

Speed is useful. Memory makes speed compound.

Post 2: A feature is also a promise

Every feature creates two products.

The first is the thing the customer sees.

The second is the promise your team now has to maintain: documentation, support, edge cases, compatibility and future decisions shaped around its existence.

That is why “Can we build it?” is rarely the best product question.

A better one is:

Are we willing to keep this promise?

Some features are worth the permanent responsibility. Others are attractive only because the first version looks small.

The cost of software is not just creation. It is continued belief.

Post 3: Useful ambition

I used to think ambitious goals had to sound enormous.

Now I think the best ambition is specific enough to guide Tuesday afternoon.

“Transform the industry” does not tell a team what to improve next.

“Cut the time it takes a customer to reach their first useful result from 40 minutes to 10” does.

The second goal may sound smaller, but it creates sharper work. It can be tested. It exposes trade-offs. It gives the team a shared definition of progress.

Ambition should expand the horizon—and improve the next decision.

Why the set works

The posts use short openings, concrete workplace observations and restrained conclusions. Each has a distinct idea while preserving the same voice: thoughtful, concise and uninterested in performance for its own sake.
